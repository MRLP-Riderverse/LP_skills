# Session Pattern: Community First, Media Later

## Core ladder

1. **Local community first:** collect small, low-pressure artifacts—photos, voice notes, event traces, introductions, questions, local business/artist profiles, and recurring check-ins.
2. **Conversational community second:** use Discord or a similar social room for responses, recognition, coordination, rough drafts, and repeated contact. Treat it as the living room, not the permanent archive.
3. **Public media third:** after artifacts accumulate, harvest them into a monthly video, series, or public recap. Media documents and amplifies community; it should not be asked to manufacture one.

## Why this matters

YouTube is a useful public doorway but a poor home for every thought. Discovery, metadata, thumbnails, and algorithmic competition make casual publishing expensive. A community space can hold imperfect signals and let people recognize one another before anything becomes polished media.

## Peer-signal research pattern

The useful question is not "What are people consuming?" but:

> What are people caring about enough to gather around, build around, make something for, or preserve?

Search for small social worlds: local exchanges, rehearsal rooms, youth enterprises, artist-run spaces, community maps, DIY scenes, bilingual festivals, maker projects, creator collectives, and low-pressure invitations.

## Synthesis rule

When several stories surface, ask whether they show:

- one broad trend;
- several distributed local pockets;
- or merely illustrative examples.

A weakly indexed culture is not the same as an inactive culture. The final brief should name this uncertainty rather than infer that no activity exists.

## Acadie.sol application

For Acadie.sol, track who is gathering, what they are making or preserving, what spaces enable participation, how online tools lead to offline contact, and what low-pressure entry points exist. The desired outcome is cultural concentration and sustained attention—not maximum global reach.
