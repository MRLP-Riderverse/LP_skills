# Ecosystem Discovery

Use this class-level skill when a user has been away from a technical or cultural ecosystem and wants to know what they may have missed. It is especially useful for crypto/Solana, creator platforms, open-source communities, and other spaces where social feeds can distort perceived importance.

## Goal
Produce a grounded landscape reset that distinguishes protocol and infrastructure progress, shipped products and consumer surfaces, community/cultural signals, and hype/speculation. Help the user re-enter without making them feel falsely current or unnecessarily behind.

## Core principles
- Social feeds are discovery surfaces, not the ecosystem itself.
- Prefer official product pages, docs, changelogs, release notes, and project announcements over reposts and commentary.
- Separate **newly visible** from **newly mature**. A feature discovered today may be beta and only weeks old.
- Look for "existing behavior + new rail" patterns: collecting, payments, subscriptions, remittances, prediction, creator commerce, and agent spending.
- Explain normalization in ordinary-user terms: what can someone understand without learning the underlying technical stack?
- Legibility is not the same as adoption, maturity, safety, or good economics. Flag speculative or gambling-adjacent mechanics.
- Treat the user's discovery gap as a media-surface problem before treating it as a knowledge deficit.

## Workflow
1. Establish the user's cutoff and absence: how long have they been away, and which social channels did they stop following?
2. Check recent official ecosystem sources and product navigation, not only news/blog pages.
3. Search for consumer-facing surfaces, docs, beta launches, hackathons, accelerators, and shipped integrations.
4. Group findings into protocol, products, culture, and cross-stack direction.
5. For each notable discovery, state what it is, why it matters, maturity/status, ordinary-user legibility, and what the user likely did or did not miss.
6. Synthesize the change in discovery mechanics: personality/feed-driven, product-driven, accelerator-driven, or documentation-driven.
7. Close with a grounded interpretation rather than hype or false reassurance.

## Solana-specific cues
For Solana re-entry research, inspect Solana official ecosystem roundups and engineering changelogs, Jupiter's product surface and documentation, validator/client/RPC/SDK/payment updates, Colosseum hackathons and accelerator cohorts, and stablecoins, tokenized assets, consumer apps, DePIN, and agentic commerce.

Jupiter is a useful example of the product-surface shift: a user may encounter swaps, perps, lending, prediction, wallets/cards, or a consumer collectible product without starting from crypto Twitter.

## Output shape
- **The short read:** one paragraph on what changed in the ecosystem's center of gravity
- **What you may have missed:** 3–7 high-signal items with dates, status, and official links
- **Why ordinary users might care:** explain the normalization path without claiming mass adoption
- **What is still hype/speculative:** identify caveats
- **Discovery diagnosis:** explain whether the gap came from social visibility, product fragmentation, or genuine novelty
- **Re-entry path:** 2–4 low-friction ways to explore without recreating the old social-feed burden

Use bullets, not tables, for Telegram readability. Keep the tone direct and humane when the user's question carries grief, regret, or comparison with people who were "picked up" by a scene.

## Pitfalls
- Do not equate the loudest X/Discord narrative with the most durable development.
- Do not present a beta as an established category.
- Do not imply the user was years behind merely because they missed a recent product launch.
- Do not call a speculative collectible, gacha, or trading mechanic normalized without explaining the risk and maturity caveat.
- Do not drown the user in a chronology. Surface the ecosystem's changed shape.
- Do not turn emotional reflection into therapy-speak; acknowledge the loss of an imagined tribe, then return agency to the user's own path.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] Each current claim has a dated primary or clearly labeled secondary source.
- [ ] Beta/preview/production status is explicit.
- [ ] Social visibility is separated from product maturity.
- [ ] Ordinary-user relevance is explained without hype.
- [ ] The answer says what the user likely did and did not miss.
- [ ] The re-entry path reduces attention burden instead of recreating social-feed dependence.
